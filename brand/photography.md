# Photography

Photography is FOTOHAVN's primary visual language. It shows the real booth, the people inside it, and the prints they take home.

Approved examples are in [references/photography/](references/photography/README.md) and [references/looks/](references/looks/README.md). All of them are images the live website uses.

## Subject priority

1. **The booth.** The Jacobean Walnut enclosure, the cream curtain, and the lit "PHOTOBOOTH" sign, ideally frontal and luminous, like the website hero.
2. **Guests**, shown candidly and respectfully: laughing inside the booth, or holding their strips in front of it.
3. **Prints**: strips held up, shared at a table, pinned to the metal board, or tucked in a pocket.
4. **Details**: the photo board, silver clips, the curtain, wood grain, and hands.

## Treatment

- Favor warm available light, soft flash, natural skin tone, and real environments, such as a mall concourse at a real event.
- Keep photographs luminous. The website hero sets type directly on the light curtain with no darkening overlay.
- Leave deliberate negative space for type. The curtain is the brand's natural canvas.
- Mix landscape, portrait, and narrow strip proportions.
- Prints may be shown as physical objects: pinned with a clip, slightly rotated, and lifted with a soft shadow. See [components.md](components.md#print-object).
- Preserve grain and material detail. Never add fake film damage, light leaks, or scratches.

## Event documentation vs. editorial imagery

- **Event documentation** comes from real Events, such as the Evia photographs and guest-board prints. It is honest, sometimes busy, and full of real people. Use it to prove the experience.
- **Editorial imagery** is composed and calm, such as the hero exterior and the candid-inside and reunion scenes. Use it to set the mood. Where an image is an illustration or approximation, say so, as the website does for its 3D booth: "An illustrated view of our booth, inspired by the real thing."

## Never

- Party stock photography, balloons, confetti, neon, or fake bokeh overlays.
- Badges, stickers, or price tags placed over a photograph.
- Generic camera icons standing in for photography.
- Blank placeholder boxes or unrelated stock imagery. Omit an optional image rather than fake it.

## Looks

FOTOHAVN offers seven photographic looks, modeled on the real booth's published outputs. Use these names and descriptions:

| Look | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Monochrome** | Black and white with deep blacks and strong contrast. |
| **Naturale** | Original colors. No filter. |
| **Soft Monochrome** | Black and white with softer contrast. |
| **Soft Color** | Slightly muted colors and softer highlights. |
| **Sepia** | Soft brown tones with faded contrast. |
| **Warm Monochrome** | Black and white with a light warm tint. |
| **Classic** | Warm colors with a golden tint. |

- Present looks in this order, which follows the published output order, unless a medium has a reason to curate a subset.
- Show looks on real photographs, ideally the same subject across looks, and preferably as printed strips.
- "Look", "photographic finish", and "filter" are all acceptable names for the set (see [voice.md](voice.md#vocabulary)).
- When one look is selected, never rely on color alone. Pair the selection mark with a text or state change.

## People and consent

- Use guest images publicly only with permission from the Event's Customer or the guests shown.
- Treat original customer media as read-only. Store crops, proxies, and derivatives separately and record their source.
- Customer-created media on the website stays fully masked in analytics. See [`website/docs/website-analytics.md`](../website/docs/website-analytics.md).

## Alt text and captions

- Write concise, specific alt text, for example "Two friends laughing together against the cream backdrop inside the FOTOHAVN booth."
- Give decorative textures empty alt text.
- Set captions in the Supporting or Meta role, aligned to the image edge.
